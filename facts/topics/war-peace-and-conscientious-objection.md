# War, Peace and Conscientious Objection

These posts trace U.S. conscription and Quaker resistance to it, from Civilian Public Service in World War II through the Vietnam-era draft, lottery and draft-card resistance, the 1948 and 1968 Richmond statements, the 1980 return of registration, and the automatic registration enacted in 2025. They also cover the Korean War and diplomacy with North Korea, the atomic bombings, the post-9/11 wars and the 2001 AUMF, U.S. military budgets, drone warfare and the Russian invasion of Ukraine, often as background for the New Conscientious Objector and Active Objection ideas.

**43 facts** checked against outside sources. Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1941–1947

Civilian Public Service (CPS) operated from 1941 to 1947 under the Selective Training and Service Act of 1940, assigning about 12,000 conscientious objectors to 'work of national importance under civilian direction' in camps run with the historic peace churches (Brethren, Friends and Mennonites).

- **Source:** [Alternative Service: Conscientious Objectors and Civilian Public Service in World War II (The National WWII Museum)](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/conscientious-objectors-civilian-public-service); [Civilian Public Service (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Public_Service)
- **In the posts:** [Draft Resister Stories](https://jeffkisling.com/2017/04/29/draft-resister-stories/) (2017-04-29); [Another Mentor, Another Quaker Jailbird](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/10/22/another-mentor-another-quaker-jailbird/) (2019-10-22)

### 2. 1942-06-04

Congress has formally declared war 11 times; the last declaration came during World War II (against Romania, June 4, 1942). Later wars, including Korea, Vietnam, Iraq and Afghanistan, were fought under congressional authorizations or resolutions rather than declarations of war.

- **Source:** [Declarations of War (U.S. Senate)](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/declarations-of-war.htm)
- **In the posts:** [Lost Ideals](https://jeffkisling.com/2017/04/08/lost-ideals/) (2017-04-08); [Peace in a culture of violence?](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/10/21/peace-in-a-culture-of-violence/) (2020-10-21); [War, Peace and the Environment](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/05/28/war-peace-and-the-environment/) (2019-05-28)

### 3. 1944–1946

Civil rights and peace activist Bayard Rustin was imprisoned from 1944 to 1946, at the federal prisons in Ashland, Kentucky, and Lewisburg, Pennsylvania, for violating the Selective Service Act as a conscientious objector.

- **Source:** [Bayard Rustin: The Inmate that the Prison Could Not Handle (National Archives (Rediscovering Black History blog))](https://rediscovering-black-history.blogs.archives.gov/2016/08/16/bayard-rustin-the-inmate-that-the-prison-could-not-handle)

### 4. 1945-08-06

The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima on August 6, 1945, and on Nagasaki on August 9, 1945, the only uses of nuclear weapons in war.

- **Source:** [Atomic Bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (National Archives (The Unwritten Record blog))](https://unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov/?p=24951)

### 5. 1948-07

A national gathering of Friends at Earlham College in Richmond, Indiana, adopted the 'Advices on Conscription and War' on July 20–22, 1948, urging Friends not to unite with any Selective Service draft, approving civil disobedience under divine compulsion, and supporting those who opposed conscription by non-registration or by registering as conscientious objectors.

- **Source:** [Advices on Conscription and War (Richmond, Indiana, 1948) (Inward Light)](https://inwardlight.org/advices-on-conscription-and-war/)

### 6. 1950-06-25

The Korean War began on June 25, 1950. Fighting stopped with the Armistice Agreement of July 27, 1953, but no peace treaty has ever been signed, so the war has never formally ended.

- **Source:** [Armistice Agreement for the Restoration of the South Korean State (1953) (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/armistice-agreement-restoration-south-korean-state)

Congress renamed Armistice Day as Veterans Day in 1954; President Eisenhower signed the change into law that year (the renaming took effect June 1, 1954).

### 8. 1959-09-23

Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev visited Iowa on September 23–24, 1959, touring Roswell Garst's farm near Coon Rapids and Iowa State University, an episode later cited as a model of citizen and agricultural diplomacy.

- **Source:** [Soviet Leader Nikita Khrushchev Visits Iowa Farm (Teaching Iowa History (State Historical Society of Iowa))](https://teachingiowahistory.org/iowa-stories/soviet-leader-nikita-khrushchev-visits-iowa-farm)

### 9. 1964-08-07

Congress passed the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution on August 7, 1964, after reported attacks on U.S. destroyers, authorizing President Lyndon Johnson to 'take all necessary measures to repel any armed attack'; later investigations found the second reported attack (August 4) likely never happened. The resolution was the legal basis for escalating the Vietnam War and was repealed in January 1971.

- **Source:** [Tonkin Gulf Resolution (1964) (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/tonkin-gulf-resolution)

### 10. 1965-07

A July 1965 Selective Service orientation memo titled 'Channeling' described using deferments as 'pressurized guidance' to steer young men into occupations the government considered to be in the national interest.

- **Source:** [Channeling (Selective Service System, July 1965) (Edward Hasbrouck)](https://hasbrouck.org/draft/channeling.html)

### 11. 1965-08

In August 1965 Congress passed, and President Johnson signed, a law making it a federal crime to destroy or mutilate a draft card, punishable by up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine.

- **Source:** [Meet the First Vietnam War Protestor to Be Prosecuted for Burning His Draft Card (TIME)](https://time.com/4061835/david-miller-draft-card); [Old Gold: Steve Smith, following his conscience (University of Iowa (Iowa Now))](https://now.uiowa.edu/2012/07/old-gold-steve-smith-following-his-conscience)

### 12. 1965-10-15

David J. Miller burned his draft card at an antiwar rally in New York City on October 15, 1965, becoming the first person prosecuted under the new draft-card law; five days later, on October 20, 1965, University of Iowa student Steve Smith burned his card at the Iowa Memorial Union, the first to do so on a college campus after the law took effect.

- **Source:** [Meet the First Vietnam War Protestor to Be Prosecuted for Burning His Draft Card (TIME)](https://time.com/4061835/david-miller-draft-card); [Old Gold: Steve Smith, following his conscience (University of Iowa (Iowa Now))](https://now.uiowa.edu/2012/07/old-gold-steve-smith-following-his-conscience)

### 14. 1967-04-28

Muhammad Ali refused induction into the U.S. Army in Houston on April 28, 1967, was convicted of violating Selective Service law that June, and had the conviction unanimously reversed by the U.S. Supreme Court in Clay v. United States on June 28, 1971.

- **Source:** [Clay v. United States (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_v._United_States)

### 15. 1968-10-11

The Friends National Conference on the Draft and Conscription, convened by the Friends Coordinating Committee on Peace at Earlham College in Richmond, Indiana, on October 11–13, 1968, issued the Richmond 'Declaration on the Draft and Conscription'. It reaffirmed the 1948 Advices, rejected draft reform ('the issue is not equal treatment under compulsion, but freedom from compulsion'), and urged resistance such as refusing to register and refusing war taxes.

- **Source:** [1968 Richmond Declaration re Draft (Inward Light)](https://inwardlight.org/1968-richmond-declaration-re-draft/); [Declaration on the Draft and Conscription (Illinois Yearly Meeting)](https://ilym.org/ilym/declaration-on-the-draft-and-conscription)

### 16. 1969-10-15

The first nationwide Moratorium to End the War in Vietnam took place on October 15, 1969, with demonstrations and teach-ins across the United States; a second Moratorium followed on November 15, 1969.

- **Source:** [Moratorium to End the War in Vietnam (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moratorium_to_End_the_War_in_Vietnam)

### 17. 1969-12-01

The first Vietnam-era draft lottery was held on December 1, 1969, at Selective Service headquarters in Washington, D.C.; birth dates drawn at random set the order of induction, replacing the earlier 'oldest first' method.

- **Source:** [Vietnam Lotteries (Selective Service System)](https://www.sss.gov/history-and-records/vietnam-lotteries/)

### 18. 1970-05-04

On May 4, 1970, Ohio National Guard soldiers fired on antiwar protesters at Kent State University, killing four students and wounding nine.
