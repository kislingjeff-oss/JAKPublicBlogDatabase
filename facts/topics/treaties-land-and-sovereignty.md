# Treaties, Land and Indigenous Sovereignty

Posts trace how Native land was taken and how it is being reclaimed: the papal bulls behind the Doctrine of Discovery, Indian removal, the Fort Laramie treaties and the seizure of the Black Hills (with the 1980 Sioux Nation ruling), the US-Dakota War and Mankato executions, allotment under the Dawes Act, and the Indian Reorganization Act. They also cover Iowa's Native history: Sauk, Meskwaki and Ioway cessions and removal, the Meskwaki land purchase of 1857, and recent land returns. Contemporary threads include UNDRIP, NDN Collective's LANDBACK campaign at Mount Rushmore, the Mashpee trust-land fight, Oak Flat, and land returns by Quakers and local governments.

**42 facts** checked against outside sources, 7 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1452-06-18

Pope Nicholas V's bull Dum Diversas (June 18, 1452) authorized King Afonso V of Portugal to invade, capture and subjugate 'Saracens and pagans', reduce them to perpetual slavery and take their property; it and the 1455 bull Romanus Pontifex are among the papal documents later grouped as the Doctrine of Discovery.

- **Source:** [Dum Diversas (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_Diversas); [Vatican formally rejects 'Doctrine of Discovery' after Indigenous calls (PBS NewsHour (AP))](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/vatican-formally-rejects-doctrine-of-discovery-after-indigenous-calls)
- **In the posts:** [A Minute to Repudiate the Doctrine Of Discovery](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/05/15/a-minute-to-repudiate-the-doctrine-of-discovery/) (2019-05-15); [A Letter to Faith Communities](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/05/13/a-letter-to-faith-communities/) (2019-05-14); [Wet’suwet’en and the law](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/02/13/wetsuweten-and-the-law/) (2020-02-13); [Spiritual Transition](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2023/05/17/spiritual-transition/) (2023-05-17)

### 2. 1493-05-04

Pope Alexander VI's bull Inter caetera (May 4, 1493) granted Spain's monarchs lands 'discovered and to be discovered' to the west; Indigenous leaders pressed Pope Francis in 2022 to rescind such bulls, and the Vatican formally repudiated the Doctrine of Discovery on March 30, 2023.

- **Source:** [Inter caetera (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_caetera); [Vatican formally rejects 'Doctrine of Discovery' after Indigenous calls (PBS NewsHour (AP))](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/vatican-formally-rejects-doctrine-of-discovery-after-indigenous-calls)
- **In the posts:** [Pope Francis Apology](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/04/02/pope-francis-apology/) (2022-04-02)

### 3. 1830-05-28

President Andrew Jackson signed the Indian Removal Act on May 28, 1830, authorizing exchanges of land and the removal of Native nations to lands west of the Mississippi River.

- **Source:** [Indian Removal Act (National Archives (DocsTeach))](https://www.docsteach.org/documents/document/indian-removal-act)
- **In the posts:** [Life and Death Struggles Continue Today](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/12/29/life-and-death-struggles-continue-today/) (2019-12-29); [Trump administration’s attack on Indian Country](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/04/13/trump-administrations-attack-on-indian-country/) (2019-04-13)

### 4. 1831

In Cherokee Nation v. Georgia (1831) the Supreme Court held that the Cherokee Nation was not a foreign state, describing tribes instead as 'domestic dependent nations'.

- **Source:** [Cherokee Nation v. Georgia, 30 U.S. 1 (1831) (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/30/1/)
- **In the posts:** [THE TREATIES OF FORT LARAMIE, 1851 AND 1868](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/05/11/the-treaties-of-fort-laramie-1851-and-1868/) (2020-05-11)

### 5. 1836

The Ioway (Báxoje) were assigned a reservation near the Great Nemaha River on the Kansas–Nebraska border in 1836, and an 1838 treaty forced them from what became Iowa.

- **Source:** [Iowa Tribe of Kansas and Nebraska (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Tribe_of_Kansas_and_Nebraska); [Honoring the Ioway Tribe of Johnson County (Our Iowa Heritage)](https://ouriowaheritage.com/honoring-the-ioway-tribe-of-johnson-county/)

### 6. 1838–1839

During the Cherokee removal (the Trail of Tears) of 1838–39, about 16,000 Cherokee were forced to walk up to 1,000 miles west; roughly 4,000 died.

> **Note:** One post (quoting other sources) gives about 17,000 removed and 2,500–6,000 deaths; estimates vary.

- **Source:** [Indian Removal Act (National Archives (DocsTeach))](https://www.docsteach.org/documents/document/indian-removal-act)
- **In the posts:** [Life and Death Struggles Continue Today](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/12/29/life-and-death-struggles-continue-today/) (2019-12-29)

### 7. 1842-10-11

In the Sac and Fox treaty of October 11, 1842, signed at Agency, Iowa, the Sauk and Meskwaki ceded their remaining Iowa lands and were required to leave Iowa by 1845 for Kansas.

- **Source:** [Sac and Fox treaty of 1842 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sac_and_Fox_treaty_of_1842)
- **In the posts:** [The Missing Map](https://channelsintoknowledge.com/2026/08/11/the-missing-map/) (2026-08-11); [Grammar of Silence](https://channelsintoknowledge.com/2026/08/09/grammar-of-silence/) (2026-08-09)

### 8. 1851-09-17

The first Treaty of Fort Laramie, signed September 17, 1851 with eight Plains nations including the Sioux, Cheyenne, Arapaho and Crow, defined tribal territories and allowed safe passage and roads; the promised $50,000 a year for 50 years was cut by the Senate to 10 years.

- **Source:** [Treaty of Fort Laramie (1851) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_Laramie_(1851))
- **In the posts:** [Treaty of Fort Laramie, 1851](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/05/11/treaty-of-fort-laramie-1851/) (2020-05-11); [THE TREATIES OF FORT LARAMIE, 1851 AND 1868](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/05/11/the-treaties-of-fort-laramie-1851-and-1868/) (2020-05-11); [Plenty Coups and Radical Hope](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/12/30/plenty-coups-and-radical-hope/) (2019-12-30)

### 9. 1857-07-13 (corrected)

After Iowa passed a law in 1856 allowing them to stay, the Meskwaki pooled their own annuity money and bought their first 80 acres in Tama County on July 13, 1857, the start of the Meskwaki Settlement.

> **Correction:** One post says the Meskwaki 'bought back Tama County land starting 1856'; 1856 was the Iowa law permitting them to remain, and the first purchase was in 1857.

### 10. 1862-08-18

The US-Dakota War began on August 18, 1862, when Dakota facing starvation and late annuity payments attacked the Lower Sioux Agency in the Minnesota River valley; it lasted about five weeks, ending September 26, 1862.

- **Source:** [Dakota War of 1862 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_War_of_1862)
- **In the posts:** [Dakota 38](https://unflinching.blog/2024/12/27/dakota-38/) (2024-12-27); [Multigenerational trauma and the US-Dakota War of 1862](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/07/07/multigenerational-trauma-and-the-us-dakota-war-of-1862/) (2022-07-07); [Scaffold](https://jeffkisling.com/2017/05/31/scaffold/) (2017-05-31)

### 11. 1862-12-26

After military trials that sentenced 303 Dakota men to death, 38 were hanged together at Mankato, Minnesota, on December 26, 1862, under President Lincoln's order, before an estimated 4,000 spectators; it remains the largest mass execution in US history.

- **Source:** [The Trials & Hanging (Minnesota Historical Society)](https://www.mnhs.org/usdakotawar/stories/history/aftermath/trials-hanging)
- **In the posts:** [Dakota 38](https://jeffkisling.com/2016/01/20/dakota-38/) (2016-01-20); [Forgive Everyone Everything](https://jeffkisling.com/2018/12/26/forgive-everyone-everything/) (2018-12-26); [Dakota 38 + 2](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/12/27/dakota-38-2/) (2022-12-27); [Mount Rushmore Anti-Trump Rally](https://jeffkisling.com/2020/07/02/mount-rushmore-anti-trump-rally/) (2020-07-03); [Multigenerational trauma and the US-Dakota War of 1862](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/07/07/multigenerational-trauma-and-the-us-dakota-war-of-1862/) (2022-07-07)

### 12. 1863-05

After the war, about 2,000 Dakota were held at Fort Snelling, and in May 1863 some 1,300 were shipped by steamboat to the Crow Creek Reservation in Dakota Territory, expelling them from Minnesota.

- **Source:** [Dakota War of 1862 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_War_of_1862)
- **In the posts:** [Dakota 38](https://unflinching.blog/2024/12/27/dakota-38/) (2024-12-27); [Multigenerational trauma and the US-Dakota War of 1862](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/07/07/multigenerational-trauma-and-the-us-dakota-war-of-1862/) (2022-07-07)

### 14. 1868-04-29 (corrected)

Article 12 of the 1868 Fort Laramie Treaty said no later cession of reservation land would be valid unless signed by three-fourths of the adult Indian men concerned.

### 15. 1868-04-29

The Fort Laramie Treaty of April 29, 1868 ended Red Cloud's War, closed the Bozeman Trail forts, and created the Great Sioux Reservation, including the Black Hills, for the Sioux's 'absolute and undisturbed use and occupation'.

- **Source:** [Fort Laramie Treaty (1868) (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/fort-laramie-treaty); [Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_Laramie_(1868))

### 16. 1868-05-07

In a second Fort Laramie treaty in May 1868, the Crow gave up about 30 million acres of their 1851 territory, keeping a reservation of about 8 million acres.

> **Note:** The post (quoting Jonathan Lear) puts the 1851 Crow territory at 33 million acres; Wikipedia's figures imply about 38 million.

- **Source:** [Crow Indian Reservation (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_Indian_Reservation)
- **In the posts:** [Plenty Coups and Radical Hope](https://jeffkisling.com/2019/12/30/plenty-coups-and-radical-hope/) (2019-12-30)

### 17. 1874–1877

In 1874 Custer's expedition confirmed gold in the Black Hills, bringing in miners; after the 1876 Battle of the Little Bighorn the US took the Black Hills in 1877.

- **Source:** [Fort Laramie Treaty (1868) (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/fort-laramie-treaty)

### 18. 1877-02-28

On February 28, 1877, Congress ratified the Manypenny Agreement, taking the Black Hills; the Sioux had been pressured by a cutoff of rations ('sell or starve').

- **Source:** [Congress ratified Manypenny Agreement on the Black Hills (JURIST)](https://www.jurist.org/thisday/2010/02/28/congress-ratified-manypenny-agreement-on-the-black-hills/); [United States v. ### 19. 1887-02-08

The Dawes (General Allotment) Act, approved February 8, 1887, split reservations into individual allotments and sold 'surplus' land to non-Indians; Indian-held land fell from about 138 million acres to 48 million acres by 1934.

- **Source:** [Dawes Act (1887) (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/dawes-act); [Dawes Act (Encyclopedia of the Great Plains (Univ. of Nebraska))](https://plainshumanities.unl.edu/encyclopedia/doc/egp.law.015.html)

### 20. 1890-12-29 (corrected)

On December 29, 1890, the US 7th Cavalry killed an estimated 250–300 Lakota, including many women and children, at Wounded Knee Creek on the Pine Ridge Reservation.

### 21. 1903-01-05

In 1867 the Medicine Lodge Treaty required three-fourths of adult men to approve land cessions; in Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock (January 5, 1903) the Supreme Court upheld Congress's breakup of the Kiowa, Comanche and Apache reservation without that consent, ruling that Congress has 'plenary authority' over tribal relations, including power to abrogate treaties.

- **Source:** [Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock, 187 U.S. 553 (1903) (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/187/553/)

### 23. 1934-06-18

### 25. 1980-06-30

In United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (June 30, 1980), the Supreme Court ruled that the 1877 seizure of the Black Hills was a taking requiring just compensation, upholding $17.1 million plus interest (about $105 million); the Sioux refused the money because accepting it would end their claim to the land, and the account now exceeds $1 billion.

- **Source:** [United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371 (1980) (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/448/371/); [United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Sioux_Nation_of_Indians)
