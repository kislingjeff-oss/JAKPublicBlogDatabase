# Indian Boarding Schools and Forced Assimilation

Posts trace US Indian boarding schools and Canadian residential schools as tools of forced assimilation, from the 1819 Civilization Fund Act and Grant's Peace Policy (with its Quaker agents and Quaker-run schools) through Pratt's Carlisle school. They follow the 2021 unmarked-grave findings in Canada, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, the Interior Department's Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative reports, FCNL and boarding-school-healing advocacy for a US truth commission, and apologies by the Pope, a US president and Quakers.

**42 facts** checked against outside sources, 1 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1819-03-03

The Civilization Fund Act of March 3, 1819 created an annual $10,000 federal fund for benevolent (largely missionary) societies to 'civilize' and educate Native peoples; the Interior Department dates federal Indian boarding school policy from this act.

- **Source:** [Civilization Fund Act (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_Fund_Act); [Secretary Haaland Announces Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative (U.S. Department of the Interior)](https://doi.gov/pressreleases/secretary-haaland-announces-federal-indian-boarding-school-initiative)
- **In the posts:** [Quaker Indigenous Residential/Boarding Schools](https://jeffkisling.com/2021/06/01/quaker-indigenous-residential-boarding-schools/) (2021-06-01); [Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative](https://landbackfriends.com/2021/06/23/federal-indian-boarding-school-initiative/) (2021-06-23); [Indian Boarding Schools: 1](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/05/13/indian-boarding-schools-1/) (2022-05-13); [Truth and Healing Commission on Indian Boarding Schools Act text](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/08/26/truth-and-healing-commission-on-indian-boarding-schools-act-text/) (2022-08-26)

### 2. 1831–1996

About 150,000 First Nations, Inuit and Métis children attended Canada's residential schools, which operated from 1831 until the last federally funded school closed in 1996.

- **Source:** [Residential Schools in Canada (The Canadian Encyclopedia)](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/residential-schools); [Canada: Hundreds of graves found at Indigenous boarding school (Al Jazeera)](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/6/24/canada-hundreds-of-graves-found-at-indigenous-boarding-school)
- **In the posts:** [Incomprehensible](https://landbackfriends.com/2021/06/25/incomprehensible/) (2021-06-25)

### 3. 1869

In an 1869 letter, Edward Shaw, a Friend from Richmond, Indiana, wrote that Quakers' work among Native peoples was meant 'to protect, to Civilize, and to Christianize our Red Brethren.'

- **Source:** [Quaker Indian Boarding Schools: Facing Our History and Ourselves (Paula Palmer) (Friends Journal)](https://www.friendsjournal.org/quaker-indian-boarding-schools/)
- **In the posts:** [Indian Boarding Schools: 1](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/05/13/indian-boarding-schools-1/) (2022-05-13); [A reckoning on Native boarding schools is long overdue](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/06/23/a-reckoning-on-native-boarding-schools-is-long-overdue/) (2022-06-23)

### 4. 1869

Under President Ulysses S. Grant's Peace Policy, begun in 1869, members of the Society of Friends recommended by Quaker bodies were appointed as superintendents and agents for Indian affairs, including the Northern and Central superintendencies.

- **Source:** [Ely Parker 1865-1879: Grant's Peace Policy (Report of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, 1869) (PBS)](https://www.pbs.org/warrior/content/timeline/hero/1869peace.html); [Quaker Indian Boarding Schools: Facing Our History and Ourselves (Paula Palmer) (Friends Journal)](https://www.friendsjournal.org/quaker-indian-boarding-schools/)

### 5. 1869–1882

Grant's Peace Policy ran from 1869 to 1882.

- **Source:** [Native American policy of the Grant administration (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_policy_of_the_Grant_administration)

### 6. 1875–1878

Fort Marion (St. Augustine, Florida) held Plains Indian prisoners of war from 1875 to 1878 under Lt. Richard Henry Pratt; in 1878 Pratt placed former prisoners as students at the Hampton Normal and Agricultural Institute in Virginia, an experiment that led to Carlisle.

- **Source:** [Prisoners from Ft. Marion (1875-1878) (Carlisle Indian School Digital Resource Center, Dickinson College)](https://carlisleindian.dickinson.edu/topics/prisoners-ft-marion-1875-1878); [Richard Henry Pratt (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Henry_Pratt)

### 7. 1879

In 1879 Commissioner of Indian Affairs Ezra Hayt directed Pratt to recruit Carlisle's first students from the Pine Ridge and Rosebud agencies 'because the children would be hostages for the good behavior of their people.'

- **Source:** [The U.S. stole generations of Indigenous children to open the West (Nick Estes) (High Country News)](https://www.hcn.org/issues/51.17/indigenous-affairs-the-us-stole-generations-of-indigenous-children-to-open-the-west)

### 8. 1879-10-06

The first group of students, including 84 Lakota children from the Rosebud and Pine Ridge agencies, arrived at Carlisle on October 6, 1879.

- **Source:** [Carlisle Indian Industrial School (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Indian_Industrial_School)

### 9. 1879–1900

Between 1879 and 1900 the Bureau of Indian Affairs opened 24 off-reservation boarding schools modeled on Carlisle.

- **Source:** [The U.S. stole generations of Indigenous children to open the West (Nick Estes) (High Country News)](https://www.hcn.org/issues/51.17/indigenous-affairs-the-us-stole-generations-of-indigenous-children-to-open-the-west)

### 10. 1879–1918

The Carlisle Indian Industrial School in Carlisle, Pennsylvania, founded by Richard Henry Pratt, operated from 1879 to 1918 as the first federally run off-reservation Indian boarding school; more than 10,000 Native children from some 140 tribes attended.

- **Source:** [Carlisle Indian Industrial School (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Indian_Industrial_School); [Richard Henry Pratt (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Henry_Pratt)

### 11. 1884

Zitkála-Šá (Gertrude Simmons Bonnin), a Yankton Dakota born in 1876, was taken at age eight by Quaker missionaries to White's Indiana Manual Labor Institute in Wabash, Indiana; she later attended Earlham College in Richmond, Indiana.

- **Source:** [Zitkála-Šá (National Park Service)](https://home.nps.gov/people/zitkala-sa.htm)

### 12. 1892

Richard Henry Pratt used the phrase 'Kill the Indian in him, and save the man' in an 1892 speech to the National Conference of Charities and Correction in Denver.

- **Source:** ["Kill the Indian in him, and save the man": R. H. Pratt on the Education of Native Americans (Carlisle Indian School Digital Resource Center, Dickinson College)](https://carlisleindian.dickinson.edu/teach/kill-indian-him-and-save-man-r-h-pratt-education-native-americans)

By 1900 about 20,000 children were in Indian boarding schools, and by 1925 that number had more than tripled.

### 15. 1913–1932

Duncan Campbell Scott, Deputy Superintendent of Canada's Department of Indian Affairs from 1913 to 1932 and a champion of the residential school system, described the government's aim as continuing 'until there is not a single Indian in Canada that has not been absorbed into the body politic.'

> **Note:** The post places the statement before a 1920 parliamentary committee; the source confirms the quotation and Scott's role but not that specific setting.

- **Source:** [Duncan Campbell Scott (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Campbell_Scott)

### 16. 1920

Duncan Campbell Scott made his statement 'Our objective is to continue until there is not a single Indian in Canada that has not been absorbed into the body politic' in 1920, while pressing Parliament for an Indian Act amendment making school attendance compulsory.

> **Note:** The source places the remark in 1920 before Parliament; it does not specify a committee.

### 17. 1973

In 1973 six-year-old Phyllis Webstad's new orange shirt was taken from her on her first day at St. Joseph Mission residential school near Williams Lake, British Columbia; her story is the origin of Orange Shirt Day.

- **Source:** [Orange Shirt Day (Brock University Library)](https://brocku.ca/library/2020/09/29/orange-shirt-day/)

### 18. 1978

Congress passed, and President Jimmy Carter signed, the Indian Child Welfare Act in 1978, giving Native parents the legal right to refuse their child's placement in a school and aiming to keep Native children in Native homes.

- **Source:** [American Indian boarding schools (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_boarding_schools)

### 24. 2016-10-01

In an October 1, 2016 Friends Journal article, Paula Palmer reported that Quakers were among the strongest promoters of the forced-assimilation policy and managed over 30 schools for Indian children, most of them boarding schools, in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries; she wrote that more than 100,000 Native children suffered the direct consequences of the boarding school policy.

- **Source:** [Quaker Indian Boarding Schools: Facing Our History and Ourselves (Paula Palmer) (Friends Journal)](https://www.friendsjournal.org/quaker-indian-boarding-schools/)

### 39. 2024-07-30

Volume 2 of the Interior Department's report, released July 30, 2024, identified 417 federal Indian boarding schools in 37 states or then-territories, at least 973 Native children who died while attending them, at least 74 burial sites at 65 school locations, and more than $23.3 billion (inflation-adjusted) in federal spending from 1871 to 1969.

- **Source:** [Secretary Haaland Announces Major Milestones for Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative (U.S. Department of the Interior)](https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/secretary-haaland-announces-major-milestones-federal-indian-boarding-school); [At least 973 Native American children died at U.S.-run boarding schools between 1871-1969 (Source NM)](https://sourcenm.com/2024/07/30/at-least-973-native-american-children-died-at-u-s-run-boarding-schools-between-1871-1969/)
