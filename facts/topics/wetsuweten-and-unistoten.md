# Wet'suwet'en, Unist'ot'en and Coastal GasLink

From January 2020 on, many posts follow the Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs' opposition to the Coastal GasLink pipeline. They cover the Unist'ot'en camp and healing centre, the Gidimt'en checkpoint, the RCMP raids of 2019, 2020 and 2021, and the 2020 solidarity rail and port blockades across Canada. They also discuss Delgamuukw, the 2020 Memorandum of Understanding, UN CERD statements, and the later prosecutions and court findings about land defenders and journalists.

**44 facts** checked against outside sources, 3 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1984-10-24

On October 24, 1984, 35 Gitxsan and 13 Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs filed the Delgamuukw (Delgamuukw-Gisday'wa) claim in the B.C. Supreme Court, seeking recognition of title and jurisdiction over about 58,000 square kilometres of northwestern British Columbia.

> **Note:** One post gives the area as 55,000 km2; most posts give 58,000 km2.

- **Source:** [Delgamuukw v British Columbia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delgamuukw_v_British_Columbia); [Delgamuukw Case (The Canadian Encyclopedia)](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/delgamuukw-case)

### 2. 1997-12-11 (corrected)

The Supreme Court of Canada decided Delgamuukw v. British Columbia on December 11, 1997. It held that Aboriginal title is an existing right protected by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, rejected the finding that it had been extinguished, and set out a test for proving title, but it ordered a new trial rather than ruling on title to the specific lands; that second trial never took place.

> **Correction:** Several posts say the Supreme Court 'confirmed' or 'proved' Wet'suwet'en and Gitxsan title to their territories; the Court laid down the legal framework and ordered a new trial, without itself deciding title to the claimed lands.

- **Source:** [Delgamuukw v British Columbia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delgamuukw_v_British_Columbia); [Delgamuukw Case (The Canadian Encyclopedia)](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/delgamuukw-case)

### 3. 2010

Freda Huson of the Unist'ot'en house group built a cabin in the path of proposed pipelines in 2010 and began hosting culture camps there, the start of the Unist'ot'en camp; The Tyee dates the Unist'ot'en checkpoint itself to April 2009.

### 4. 2015

The three-storey Unist'ot'en Healing Centre, a land-based healing facility, was built in 2015 at kilometre 66 of the Morice West Forest Service Road near the Morice River (Wedzin Kwa).

### 5. 2017

The RCMP's Community-Industry Response Group (C-IRG), formed in 2017 to police resource-extraction conflicts, carried out the Coastal GasLink enforcement actions and became the subject of an investigation by the Civilian Review and Complaints Commission for the RCMP.

- **Source:** [RCMP Actions on Wet'suwet'en Territory (The Tyee)](https://thetyee.ca/News/2024/01/17/RCMP-Actions-Wetsuweten-Territory/)

### 6. 2018-10-01

LNG Canada, a Shell-led joint venture, made its final investment decision on October 1, 2018, for a roughly $40 billion liquefied natural gas export project at Kitimat, B.C., which the Coastal GasLink pipeline was built to supply.
