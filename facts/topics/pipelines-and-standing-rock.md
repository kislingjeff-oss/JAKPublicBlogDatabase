# Dakota Access, Keystone XL and Pipeline Resistance

The posts follow the campaigns against oil and carbon pipelines from 2013 to 2025. They cover the Keystone XL Pledge of Resistance and Keystone XL's rejection, revival and cancellation; the Dakota Access Pipeline's route, Iowa permits and eminent domain, the Standing Rock camps, and the federal and Iowa court cases; Line 3; and the proposed Summit and Navigator CO2 pipelines in Iowa.

**42 facts** checked against outside sources, 1 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 2013

In summer 2013 the Pledge of Resistance organizers ran weekend trainings for local 'action leaders' in 25 U.S. cities, teaching them to plan nonviolent direct actions and to train others.

- **Source:** [The Keystone XL Pledge of Resistance takes off (Waging Nonviolence)](https://wagingnonviolence.org/feature/the-keystone-xl-pledge-of-resistance-takes-off/)
- **In the posts:** [Significance of President Obama’s Keystone Decision](https://jeffkisling.com/2015/11/07/significance-of-president-obamas-keystone-decision/) (2015-11-07); [Keystone XL Pipeline Permit Canceled](https://jeffkisling.com/2021/01/21/keystone-xl-pipeline-permit-canceled/) (2021-01-21)

### 2. 2013

The Keystone XL Pledge of Resistance, organized by CREDO, the Rainforest Action Network and The Other 98%, launched in spring 2013; signers pledged to risk arrest in civil disobedience if the Obama administration moved to approve the Keystone XL pipeline, and more than 94,000 people had signed by April 2014.

> **Note:** The posts give a final total of over 97,000 signers and a launch date of March 6, 2013; the sources checked confirm spring 2013 and more than 94,000 signers by April 2014, but not those exact figures.

- **Source:** [The Keystone XL Pledge of Resistance takes off (Waging Nonviolence)](https://wagingnonviolence.org/feature/the-keystone-xl-pledge-of-resistance-takes-off/); [Decentralized Actions Ready to Stop Keystone XL (Rainforest Action Network)](https://www.ran.org/the-understory/decentralized_actions_ready_to_stop_keystone_xl/)
- **In the posts:** [Significance of President Obama’s Keystone Decision](https://jeffkisling.com/2015/11/07/significance-of-president-obamas-keystone-decision/) (2015-11-07); [Keystone XL Pipeline Permit Canceled](https://jeffkisling.com/2021/01/21/keystone-xl-pipeline-permit-canceled/) (2021-01-21); [Keystone XL is dead](https://jeffkisling.com/2021/06/10/keystone-xl-is-dead/) (2021-06-10); [Lessons Learned from the Keystone Pledge of Resistance](https://jeffkisling.com/2018/06/05/lessons-learned-from-the-keystone-pledge-of-resistance/) (2018-06-05)

### 3. 2015

Bold Iowa was founded in 2015 to oppose the Dakota Access Pipeline; its founder and director is Ed Fallon, a former Iowa state representative.

> **Note:** Wikipedia's Ed Fallon article dates his direction of Bold Iowa from 2016; Bold Iowa's own press release says it was founded in 2015.

- **Source:** [Response to DAPL Shutdown (Bold Iowa)](http://boldiowa.com/response-to-dapl-shutdown/)

### 4. 2015-11-06

On November 6, 2015, President Barack Obama rejected TransCanada's application for the Keystone XL pipeline after the State Department concluded it would not serve the national interest of the United States.

- **Source:** [President Obama rejects Keystone XL pipeline plan (MPR News)](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/11/06/keystone-xl-pipeline)

### 5. 2016-03-10

On March 10, 2016, the Iowa Utilities Board voted 3–0 to grant Dakota Access a permit for a crude oil pipeline crossing nearly 350 miles and 18 Iowa counties, and allowed the company to use eminent domain on land where owners would not grant easements voluntarily.

- **Source:** [Iowa Utilities Board Approves Oil Pipeline (Tri States Public Radio)](https://www.tspr.org/tspr-local/2016-03-10/iowa-utilities-board-approves-oil-pipeline); [Dakota Access Pipeline (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Access_Pipeline)

### 11. 2016–2017

The Dakota Access Pipeline, built by Energy Transfer at a cost of about $3.8 billion, runs about 1,172 miles from the Bakken oil fields of North Dakota through South Dakota and Iowa to an oil hub near Patoka, Illinois.
