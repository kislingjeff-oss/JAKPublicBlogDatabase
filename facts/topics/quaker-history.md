# Quaker History and Institutions

The posts often cite Quaker history: George Fox and the early persecuted Friends, the Peace Testimony of 1660, Quaker antislavery work (Woolman, Benezet, Philadelphia Yearly Meeting's rulings, Coffin, Mott), and the 19th-century separations. They also cover Iowa Quaker institutions (Bear Creek Meeting, the 1877 founding of Iowa Yearly Meeting (Conservative), Scattergood Friends School and its 1939–43 refugee hostel) and national bodies such as AFSC, FCNL, Toward Right Relationship with Native Peoples and Quaker Social Change Ministry.

**42 facts** checked against outside sources, 4 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1651 (corrected)

In 1651, while held in Derby, George Fox refused a captaincy offered to him in the Commonwealth army, saying he 'lived in the virtue of that life and power that took away the occasion of all wars.'

> **Correction:** One post dates this statement to 1650; Fox's imprisonment at Derby began in 1650, but the captaincy offer and his refusal came in 1651. Other posts quote the words without a date.

- **Source:** [Take Away the Occasion of All Wars (Daily Quaker)](https://dailyquaker.com/2025/04/take-away-the-occasion-of-all-wars/); [George Fox (Quakers in the World)](https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/12)
- **In the posts:** [U.S. doesn’t care about endless war](https://jeffkisling.com/2017/10/19/u-s-doesnt-care-about-endless-war/) (2017-10-19); [Life without a car](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2022/10/09/life-without-a-car/) (2022-10-09); [Discerning Peace and Social Concerns](https://quakersandreligioussocialism.com/2023/06/04/discerning-peace-and-social-concerns/) (2023-06-04); [We are called to take away the occasion of all wars and empires](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2026/01/02/we-are-called-to-take-away-the-occasion-of-all-wars-and-empires/) (2026-01-02)

### 2. 1652–1689

Quakers date the beginning of their movement in England to 1652; an estimated 15,000 British Friends were persecuted for their faith between 1652 and the arrival of freedom of worship in 1687–89.

- **Source:** [Prisoners of Conscience (Quakers in the World)](https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/44)
- **In the posts:** [Persecution](https://unflinching.blog/2025/01/25/persecution/) (2025-01-25); [Decolonial Path for Quakers 8 – The other side of the paradox](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/09/05/decolonial-path-for-quakers-8-the-other-side-of-the-paradox/) (2025-09-05)

### 3. 1656

In 1656, writing from prison at Launceston in Cornwall, George Fox urged Friends to 'walk cheerfully over the world, answering that of God in every one.'

- **Source:** [Quaker Faith & Practice, passage 19.32 (Britain Yearly Meeting)](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/19-32/)
- **In the posts:** [Walk Cheerfully Over the World](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2026/03/07/walk-cheerfully-over-the-world/) (2026-03-07); [Walk Cheerfully Over the World](https://activeobjection.substack.com/p/walk-cheerfully-over-the-world) (2026-05-31); [First Nation – Farmer Climate Unity March: An Introduction](https://firstnationfarmer.com/2020/04/04/first-nation-farmer-climate-unity-march-an-introduction/) (2020-04-04)

### 4. 1657

In 1657 George Fox addressed an epistle 'to Friends beyond sea, that have Blacks and Indian slaves,' telling them the gospel brings liberty and freedom to every captive.

> **Note:** The posts carry this in a speech by Rev. William Barber, who called it Fox challenging slaveholders.

- **Source:** [Quakers & Slavery: George Fox (Bryn Mawr College / Tri-College Libraries)](https://web.tricolib.brynmawr.edu/speccoll/quakersandslavery/commentary/people/fox.php)

### 5. 1660

In 1660 George Fox and other Friends sent King Charles II a declaration denying 'all outward wars, and strife, and fightings with outward weapons, for any end, or under any pretence whatsoever'. It is the foundational statement of the Quaker Peace Testimony.

- **Source:** [The Quaker Peace Testimony (Minneapolis Friends Meeting (quaker.org))](https://quaker.org/legacy/minnfm/peace/)
- **In the posts:** [Study Guide: The NewCO](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/10/30/study-guide-the-newco/) (2025-10-30)

### 6. 1660–1661

In 1660, 535 Friends from York and Yorkshire were imprisoned in just two months, and in 1661, 120 Friends from a single Gloucester meeting were arrested on one day.

- **Source:** [Prisoners of Conscience (Quakers in the World)](https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/44)
- **In the posts:** [Persecution](https://unflinching.blog/2025/01/25/persecution/) (2025-01-25)

### 7. 1661

New England Yearly Meeting of Friends was founded in 1661.

- **Source:** [New England Yearly Meeting (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Yearly_Meeting)
- **In the posts:** [Quaker suit against ICE entering houses of worship](https://unflinching.blog/2025/01/28/quaker-suit-against-ice-entering-houses-of-worship/) (2025-01-28)

### 8. 1664

England's Conventicle Act of 1664 forbade religious gatherings of more than five people other than Church of England services. Within a year of its passage, 2,100 Quakers were arrested from just five London meetings.

- **Source:** [Prisoners of Conscience (Quakers in the World)](https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/44)
- **In the posts:** [Persecution](https://unflinching.blog/2025/01/25/persecution/) (2025-01-25)

### 9. 1696

In 1696 William Southeby presented a testimony against slavery at a Friends yearly meeting.

> **Note:** Posts say he called for a ban on Quakers owning and importing slaves; the archival record seen describes his remarks only as a testimony against slavery.

- **Source:** [William Southbe Testimony Against Slavery 1696 (Historical Society of Pennsylvania)](https://digitallibrary.hsp.org/index.php/Detail/objects/14675)
- **In the posts:** [Our Moral Moment Comes to Congress](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/04/28/our-moral-moment-comes-to-congress/) (2025-04-28)

### 10. 1713–1784

Anthony Benezet (1713–1784), a Quaker schoolteacher in Philadelphia, wrote widely circulated antislavery tracts, among them 'Observations on the Inslaving, Importing and Purchasing of Negroes' and 'Some Historical Account of Guinea' (1772).

- **Source:** [Anthony Benezet (Quakers in the World)](https://www.quakersintheworld.org/quakers-in-action/60)

### 11. 1754

John Woolman's antislavery essay 'Some Considerations on the Keeping of Negroes' was published in 1754 with the approval of Philadelphia Yearly Meeting.

> **Note:** Wikipedia's article gives 1753; the Bryn Mawr Quakers & Slavery commentary gives 1754, the date the posts use.

- **Source:** [Quakers & Slavery: John Woolman (Bryn Mawr College / Tri-College Libraries)](https://web.tricolib.brynmawr.edu/speccoll/quakersandslavery/commentary/people/woolman.pdf); [John Woolman (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Woolman)

### 12. 1758

John Woolman travelled widely and persuaded many Quaker slaveholders to free the people they enslaved. In 1758 Philadelphia Yearly Meeting banned members from buying and selling slaves.

- **Source:** [Quakers & Slavery: John Woolman (Bryn Mawr College / Tri-College Libraries)](https://web.tricolib.brynmawr.edu/speccoll/quakersandslavery/commentary/people/woolman.pdf); [John Woolman (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Woolman)

### 13. 1763 (corrected)

In 1763 John Woolman travelled to Wyalusing on the Susquehanna River to visit the Delaware (Lenape) people, writing that 'love was the first motion' of the journey.

> **Correction:** Posts quote the line as 'Let Love be the first motion'; Woolman's journal reads 'Love was the first motion.' Another post quotes his related words about long feeling love toward 'the Natives of this Land.'

### 14. 1772–1793

John Woolman died of smallpox in York, England, in 1772. His essay 'A Word of Remembrance and Caution to the Rich', which asks readers to see whether 'the seeds of war' are nourished by their possessions, was published after his death, in a 1793 edition.

### 15. 1776

In 1776 Philadelphia Yearly Meeting forbade its members to own slaves, and members who did not free the people they enslaved could be disowned.

- **Source:** [History of slavery in Pennsylvania (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_Pennsylvania); [Quakers & Slavery: John Woolman (Bryn Mawr College / Tri-College Libraries)](https://web.tricolib.brynmawr.edu/speccoll/quakersandslavery/commentary/people/woolman.pdf)

### 16. 1821–1839

Benjamin Lundy, a Quaker abolitionist, founded the antislavery paper 'Genius of Universal Emancipation' in 1821 and died in 1839.

- **Source:** [Benjamin Lundy (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Lundy)
- **In the posts:** [Our Moral Moment Comes to Congress](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/04/28/our-moral-moment-comes-to-congress/) (2025-04-28)

### 17. 1826–1877

Levi Coffin, a Quaker abolitionist, ran a major Underground Railroad station at Newport (now Fountain City), Indiana, from 1826 and later in Cincinnati. He was called the 'President of the Underground Railroad' and estimated that he helped about 3,000 freedom seekers.

> **Note:** Many other posts say only a minority of Quakers took part in the Underground Railroad; that is not checked here.

- **Source:** [Levi Coffin (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Coffin)
- **In the posts:** [Quakers and the Poor People’s Campaign](https://jeffkisling.com/2018/07/06/quakers-and-the-poor-peoples-campaign/) (2018-07-06); [Our Moral Moment Comes to Congress](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/04/28/our-moral-moment-comes-to-congress/) (2025-04-28)

### 18. 1827–1854

Orthodox and Hicksite Friends separated in 1827–28. A second division, between Wilburite (Conservative) and Gurneyite (evangelical) Friends, began in Rhode Island in 1842 and became lasting after the Ohio split of 1854.

> **Note:** The posts name the Hicksite, Orthodox, Wilburite and Gurneyite branches but do not give these earlier dates.

- **Source:** [Conservative Friends (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Friends)
- **In the posts:** [Some Quaker history regarding residential schools](https://landbackfriends.com/2021/06/29/some-quaker-history-regarding-residential-schools/) (2021-06-29); [Study Guide: The NewCO](https://newconscientiousobjector.com/2025/10/30/study-guide-the-newco/) (2025-10-30)

### 19. 1848

Lucretia Mott, a Hicksite Quaker minister and leading abolitionist, organized the 1848 Seneca Falls women's rights convention with Elizabeth Cady Stanton.

- **Source:** [Lucretia Mott (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucretia_Mott)

### 20. 1850s (corrected)

Bear Creek Friends Meeting is in Adams Township, Dallas County, Iowa, about three miles north of Earlham. Friends from Indiana began settling in the area in the early 1850s.

> **Correction:** Most posts say the meeting is north of Earlham. One post ('The Missing Map') places it in Madison County, the county Earlham is in, but the meetinghouse is in Dallas County.

### 21. 1877

In 1877, after revival meetings brought evangelical practices into the Bear Creek area, conservative members organized a separate meeting on June 30, 1877. On September 7, 1877, they established Iowa Yearly Meeting of (Conservative) Friends, one of three Conservative yearly meetings, with Ohio and North Carolina.

- **Source:** [Bear Creek Friends Meeting House (IAGenWeb, Madison County, Iowa)](https://iagenweb.org/madison/churches/bear_creek.html); [Conservative Friends (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Friends)

### 22. 1890

Scattergood Friends School was founded in 1890 near West Branch, Iowa, by Iowa's Wilburite (Conservative) Quakers. It is owned and run by Iowa Yearly Meeting of Friends (Conservative).

- **Source:** [Scattergood Friends School (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattergood_Friends_School); [Our History (Scattergood Friends School)](https://www.scattergood.org/?p=379)

### 23. 1917-04-30

The American Friends Service Committee was founded on April 30, 1917, to give conscientious objectors in World War I a constructive alternative to military service. Its volunteers did relief and rebuilding work in France.

- **Source:** [American Friends Service Committee (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Friends_Service_Committee)

### 26. 1939–1943

From 1939 to 1943 the Scattergood campus was the AFSC's Scattergood Hostel, leased to the AFSC by Iowa Quakers for one dollar a year. It housed about 185–186 refugees from Nazi-occupied Europe and helped them resettle in the United States.

> **Note:** Sources differ slightly: Scattergood gives 186 guests, and Wikipedia gives 185.
