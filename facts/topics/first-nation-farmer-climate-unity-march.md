# First Nation–Farmer Climate Unity March and Iowa Organizing

Posts recount the September 2018 First Nation–Farmer Climate Unity March, a walk of about 94 miles along the Dakota Access Pipeline route from Des Moines to Fort Dodge organized by Bold Iowa and Indigenous Iowa, and the landowner/Sierra Club eminent-domain case the march supported. They also follow the Iowa organizing that came after it: Great Plains Action Society (formerly Indigenous Iowa), Truthsgiving, the Buffalo Rebellion coalition, resistance to CO2 pipelines, and GPAS's Urban Resilience Hub in Iowa City, with earlier context from Keystone XL resistance and the Ponca Trail of Tears.

**38 facts** checked against outside sources, 1 of them corrected (the posts had a detail slightly wrong; the corrected version is shown). Each fact lists the source that confirms it and the posts that state it. The wording is a plain summary, not a quotation from the posts.

## Facts

### 1. 1877

The Ponca Tribe was forcibly removed from northern Nebraska in 1877 along a route now known as the Ponca Trail of Tears; Chief Standing Bear's grave lies in that region.

### 2. 2014

Ed Fallon of Bold Iowa took part in the 2014 Great March for Climate Action, an eight-month, roughly 3,100-mile walk from Los Angeles to Washington, D.C., which he later chronicled in his memoir 'Marcher, Walker, Pilgrim' (launched in December 2018).

### 4. 2014-09-27

The Harvest the Hope concert against Keystone XL, headlined by Neil Young and Willie Nelson, was held on September 27, 2014, on farmland near Neligh, Nebraska, that lies on the historic Ponca Trail of Tears; more than 8,500 people attended.
